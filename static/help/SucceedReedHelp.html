SucceedReed Help

Q: What is SucceedReed for?

A: SucceedReed is a site for caring for woodwind instrument reeds. Reeds require specific amounts of rest and a “break-in” process when you first open them. If you do not pay the required attention to these processes, then your reed may die prematurely and not sound as well in performance. Furthermore, it can be difficult to keep track of the rest times, break-in processes, and what reeds are in best health for performance. Many performers may have 10-20 reeds in rotation, which may get complex. This website, while in its MVP phase, provides a simple and hassle-free way to keep track of reeds simply by logging sessions for the reeds.

Q: What are “break-in” reeds?

A: Break-in reeds are reeds that have just been opened, and need to be adjusted to playing before the performer can begin regular usage. This way, the reed will last longer and receive proper care. To keep track of this, the website breaks down the break-in process into 5 “steps”. The step changes when a session is logged, so it takes 5 sessions for a reed to be fully broken in. Each session is associated with a recommended playing time to ease the reed into regular use. If the reed is overplayed, the health score will be decreased.

The site is built off of this model:

Step 2: 5 minutes

Step 3: 10 minutes

Step 4: 15 minutes

Step 5: 20 minutes

After this, the reed will be removed from the break-in table and moved to the recommended table.

Q: What are recommended reeds and the best reeds?

A: The recommended reeds are all the reeds that are in regular usage. They are sorted in the table by health score and the ones with the most rest are put at the top. The best recommended reed is the one that has been “rested” adequately, and has the highest health score. If there are no rested reeds, then the best reed is the one with the highest health score. For the best break-in reed, it is simply the one that has rested the most days.

Q: What does it mean for a reed to be “rested” ?

For a reed to be rested, it has to have not been played for at least 1 day. 1 day is the absolute minimum required rest for a reed and playing it too often will result in a premature death. This number is recommended by most sources. The rest attribute is how many days the reed has not been played, this number is used to determine if it is rested.

First, the “weights” and “weighted ratings” of the sessions are totaled up. These weights are necessary because a practice session that occurred a week ago should be considered more than a session 3 months ago. This is modeled using the exponential decay function y = . The weighted rating is then determined using a convert system above, where a “happy face” = 2, “straight face” = 0, “mad face” = -2. Think of a 10 star rating system where bad would be 0/10, ok would be 5/10 and great would be 10/10. This number is then multiplied by the session's weight, as described above, and is multiplied by an overplayed factor. If a reed goes above the recommended playing time (for regular reeds it is 40 minutes, for break-in reeds see break-in process above), it is either “Very Overplayed” or “Slightly Overplayed”. If it is “Very Overplayed”, meaning the total session minutes is above 175% of the recommended time, then the rating is multiplied by .8. If it is “Slightly Overplayed”, meaning the session minutes is under 175%, but still over the recommended time, then the rating is multiplied by .9.

Finally, the health score is calculated by taking the total of the ratings divided by their weights and then it is normalized to get a percentage out of 100.

Q: What do each of the reed attributes mean?:

A:

Id: A 4 letter max identifier of your choosing used to identify your reed. I recommend using a letter number system. For example, you may list your jazz reeds as J1, J2, J3, and your classical reeds as C1, C2, C3. It can be whatever you please and whatever is easiest for you to identify. I recommend writing the identifier on the case of your reed or the reed itself to keep track of it.

Type: This is the type of your reed depending on the type of reed you bought. Examples include Vandoren V16, Red Box, D’Addario Select Jazz, etc. It could simply be labeled as ‘Jazz’ or ‘Classical’ as well.

Strength: This should be listed on the box of your reed and is usually a number or decimal from 1-5. Common numbers include 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5, etc.

Break-in Date: This is simply the date that you first added the reed.

Step: See break-in process question above. The step is a number from 1-5 if it is in the break-in process, and “None” if the reed is fully broken in.

Minutes Played: The duration of all the sessions combined. This is the total time the reed was played.

Rested: If the reed has not been played for at least a day, it is rested.

Recommended Time: The maximum duration of time the reed should be played before it is rested. If it is played over this time, the health is decreased.

Days Last Played: The time since the latest practice session.
